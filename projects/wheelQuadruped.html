Skating Quadruped: Passive Braked Wheels for Efficient Locomotion

My own locomotion idea: add four passive, brakeable wheels to a point-foot quadruped and move by gliding, braking and stepping. Across 720 paired trials on three robot models it cut cost of transport by 35% versus walking (p = 5.6e-30).

Legged robots are versatile but expensive to run: every step costs energy. Wheel-legged robots are efficient on flat ground but need a motor in every wheel. My idea sits in between — give a 12-DoF point-foot quadruped four passive wheels that can only be braked. By locking and releasing the wheels in time with the gait, the robot pushes off, glides and brakes: it skates instead of walking.

I compared the skating gait against point-foot walking and against hub-motor wheel-legged designs on three quadruped models and a range of terrains, with hand-written controllers and a PPO policy, and tested every comparison with paired statistics.

Results

−35.3%
cost of transport vs point-foot walking (172/172 paired wins, p = 5.6e-30)
−45.1%
vs hub-motor wheel-legs (178/179 paired wins, p = 1.1e-30)
720
paired trials on 3 robot models (Wilcoxon signed-rank)
1.05 m/s
learned skating speed (PPO) vs 0.59 m/s scripted

Highlights

  • No stability penalty: 5/180 falls while skating vs 6/180 for point-foot walking.
  • Mapped the working envelope — slopes up to ±16°, friction 0.12–0.90, roughness up to 75 mm and speeds of 0.4–2.0 m/s — and documented where it fails: +20° slopes, gravel and stairs.
  • Simulator rigour before trusting any number: found and fixed a PhysX brake leak via joint armature (drift 12.73 → 0.11 mm) and a reward exploit in the RL setup.
  • A PPO policy with reference-state initialisation learned to skate almost twice as fast as the scripted controller.
