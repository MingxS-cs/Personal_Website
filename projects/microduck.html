Microduck — Sim-to-Real RL for a 1 kg Biped

Rebuilt the open-source Microduck biped around 15 HX-12H bus servos, wrote its calibration and sensorless-force tooling, trained walking policies in Isaac Lab and ran them on the real robot — then traced a fall to actuation latency the simulator never modelled.

Microduck is a small open-source bipedal robot. I rebuilt it around stronger Hiwonder HX-12H bus servos, wrote the tooling needed to assemble and calibrate it, trained walking policies in Isaac Lab and deployed them on the real robot.

It does not walk unassisted yet — but the first floor test showed exactly why: the real actuation chain is 2.5 control ticks slower than the simulator assumed. Closing that gap is the current work.

Hardware redesign

  • Chose servos by torque margin: HX-06L would run the ankle at 73% of stall and HX-08L needed a 453 mm robot; HX-12H gives about twice the torque in the same footprint, leaving the ankle at 27% of stall at 1.05× scale.
  • Strictly proportional 1.05× (262 mm) and 1.10× (275 mm) assemblies from a layout solver, with solid-Boolean interference checks across five poses — every contact under a millimetre.

Tooling

  • STEP → URDF from geometry (16 links, 15 joints), fixing 13 of 16 non-positive-definite inertia tensors, plus self-collision limit scans — 123 passing tests.
  • Provisioning web app: an assembly wizard (bulk homing and a whole-robot health check) and a joint calibration bench (ID mapping, direction, zero and limits taught by back-driving).
  • Sensorless force feedback: the HX-12H cannot read current, so torque is inferred from voltage-compensated following error plus friction — R² 0.999 and a ±0.061 N·m deadband in simulation, enabling contact detection, drag-to-teach and virtual springs.

RL training

  • Isaac Lab + rsl_rl PPO with 4,096 parallel environments on an RTX 4090; a 41-D observation of gyro, gravity, command, quantised joint positions, bus-estimated velocity, last action and gait phase.
  • Domain randomisation of head payload, CoM, inertia, pushes, IMU mount error and observation lag; about 14 training rounds turned a jittery 8 Hz gait into a 1.8 Hz walk and halved falls under pushes.

Sim-to-real

  • The ONNX policy runs at 18.2 Hz from a laptop over a USB-serial servo bus, with a 100 Hz IMU on the robot's back.
  • First unassisted floor test: roll oscillation grew at the gait frequency (±1.5° → ±24°) until the robot fell — even though the hardware chain was clean (0 timeouts, 55 ms ticks).
  • Root cause: the simulator modelled only observation delay. On hardware each action waits for a bus write plus 55 ms of servo interpolation — 2.5 ticks instead of ~1 — pushing the loop from stabilisable (p·Td 0.49) to unstabilisable (1.23).
  • With randomised action delay, a checkpoint walks under the full real delay in simulation (1.80 Hz, 17 mm foot lift). Longer training then collapsed to standing still — a reward-pricing trap — so the next steps are a delay curriculum and splitting the servo bus to cut latency.
