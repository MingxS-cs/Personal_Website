Do Distribution Metrics Predict Robot Success? Drifting vs Diffusion Policies

Benchmarked one-step Drifting Models against regression, a 100-step diffusion policy and BeT on BlockPush and Franka cube stacking — and showed that replaying expert chunks wins every distributional metric while closed-loop success collapses.

Drifting Models (arXiv 2602.04770) generate a sample in a single step, where diffusion policies need dozens. I set out to test whether that makes them better behaviour-cloning policies for multimodal robot tasks — and along the way asked a more basic question: do the distributional metrics we use to judge such policies actually predict closed-loop success?

Across 794 training and evaluation runs I compared MSE regression, Drifting, a 100-step diffusion policy, BeT and retrieval baselines.

Working title: “A Lookup Table Beats Every Policy We Trained”

Findings

  • A lookup table wins on paper: replacing 90% of a frozen policy's actions with copied expert chunks tops every distributional metric — 62× closer to the demonstration manifold, coverage 0.717, 25.9 effective modes — while task success collapses (paired t = −9.3).
  • k-means action vocabularies encode motion phase rather than task mode (η² 0.885 vs 0.001), so "mode coverage" built on them measures the wrong thing.
  • Training on borrowed expert targets hurts (−0.129 ± 0.006 success, t = −22.7, 3 seeds), and Drifting showed no closed-loop gain over plain regression on either benchmark.
  • Ten earlier claims were withdrawn after ablations and are documented in the write-up — negative results are reported as carefully as positive ones.
